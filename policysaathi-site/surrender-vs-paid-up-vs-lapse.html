Surrender vs Paid-up vs Lapse

Premiums becoming hard to pay? There are three ways to stop a policy — each has a very different outcome.

Many people simply “close the policy” when premiums get tough. But there are three distinct paths, and each returns a different amount. Understand them first:

Aspect Surrender Paid-up Lapse
Money now Yes (GSV/SSV) No — paid at maturity No
Life cover Ends Reduced, but continues Ends (until revived)
Payout at maturity Nothing Yes (reduced sum assured + bonus) Nothing (or very little surrender value)
Revive later? No (you need a new policy) No (stays paid-up) Yes — within the revival period (2–5 years)
When to use You need money now You don’t need money now but will later Never — the worst option

How is paid-up value calculated?

The formula is simple (using IRDAI’s 2024 minimum):

Reduced sum assured = Original sum assured × (years of premium paid ÷ total premium paying term)

Example: ₹5,00,000 sum assured, 15-year term, 5 years paid → 5/15 × ₹5,00,000 = ₹1,66,667 at maturity (bonus extra). Because the policy keeps participating in bonuses, paid-up often beats surrender when you don’t need money immediately.

Why is lapse the worst?

Lapse means: you stopped paying premiums and didn’t convert the policy to paid-up either. In that case:

  • Life cover ends
  • Nothing is paid at maturity
  • Some policies reduce the surrender value too
  • Reviving later requires interest payments and a fresh health declaration

If you can’t pay, at least request paid-up conversion — it is free.

A practical rule

  1. Need money immediately? Check a policy loan first (up to 80–90% of surrender value). If that’s not possible, surrender.
  2. Need money in 5–10 years? Go paid-up — you will get more than surrendering.
  3. Some time, and no loan needed? Ask about reducing the premium paying term — some policies allow it.
Important: Before taking any step, ask your insurer in writing for both a “surrender quote” and a “paid-up projection” — decide with both numbers in front of you.

Questions

Does a paid-up policy still earn bonuses?

Yes. Participating (with-profit) policies continue to receive declared bonuses after becoming paid-up — on the reduced sum assured. At maturity you get the reduced sum assured plus accumulated bonuses.

Can I buy insurance again after surrendering?

Yes, but the new policy comes with new premium rates and a medical test. Premiums rise with age, and existing health conditions can make acceptance difficult. Think before surrendering.

How long do I have to revive a lapsed policy?

Usually 2 to 5 years. Revival requires paying pending premiums with interest and a fresh health declaration. If the revival period passes, you may get very little or nothing.
