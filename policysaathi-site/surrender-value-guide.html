What is surrender value?

When you close a life insurance policy before maturity, the money you get back is called the surrender value. Here is the full picture in plain English.

In one line: Closing a life insurance policy before maturity = surrender. The money the insurer pays back at that point = surrender value.

When does surrender value start?

For regular premium traditional policies, surrender value begins after 2 full years of premiums are paid. Close the policy within the first year and you usually get nothing.

Under IRDAI’s 2024 rules, some policies may qualify for a special surrender value earlier. The safest check: look for the “Surrender Value” section in your policy document.

There are two kinds of surrender value

1. GSV — Guaranteed Surrender Value

This is the legal minimum. IRDAI fixes the minimum percentage insurers must pay:

Policy yearMinimum GSV
2nd year30% of premiums paid
3rd year35% of premiums paid
4th–7th year50% of premiums paid
After the 7th yearRises progressively, up to 90%
Final 2 yearsAt least 90%

2. SSV — Special Surrender Value

The insurer calculates this itself, based on the policy’s benefits, bonuses and remaining years. SSV is often higher than GSV.

The insurer pays whichever is higher. So treat GSV as the floor, not the final answer.

A worked example

Ramesh bought a 15-year endowment policy with a ₹50,000 annual premium. After 5 years he needed money:

  • Total premiums paid = ₹2,50,000
  • 5th year → the 4–7 year bracket → minimum 50%
  • GSV = ₹1,25,000
  • If the insurer’s SSV works out to ₹1,45,000, Ramesh receives ₹1,45,000
That means paying ₹2,50,000 and getting back ₹1,25,000–₹1,45,000 — surrendering usually means a loss. Ask yourself first: do I need money now, or can I leave the policy paid-up?

There is also a policy loan

Most traditional policies allow a loan of up to 80–90% of the surrender value. The advantage: the policy stays active, the life cover stays, and you still get money. Always check this option before surrendering.

How to surrender

  1. Get the surrender form from the insurer’s website or branch
  2. Submit the form + original policy + bank details + KYC
  3. The insurer calculates and gives you a surrender quote
  4. Accept the quote → money reaches your bank in 7–15 working days

What you lose by surrendering

  • A large part of your premiums (the most in the early years)
  • Your life cover — replacement cover costs more at an older age
  • Your long-term financial goal is interrupted
  • Tax benefits may be affected — check with your CA

Surrender questions

Is surrender value taxable?

For traditional policies, surrender value is generally not taxed if the premiums fall within the 80C limit and policy conditions are met. For ULIPs with annual premiums above ₹2.5 lakh, gains are taxable. This is general information — confirm with your CA.

What other options do I have besides surrendering?

Three options: surrender (money now, policy ends), paid-up (stop premiums, smaller payout at maturity, cover continues), lapse (stop everything, usually nothing). See the full comparison here.

What happens if I surrender a ULIP?

Before 5 years: IRDAI’s minimum table applies (GSV). After 5 years: you get the current value of your funds — which may be lower or higher depending on the market.

Where is the surrender value table in my policy document?

Near the end of the policy, under “Surrender Value” or “Non-forfeiture”. It lists the percentage for each year — that is your insurer’s actual figure, more accurate than any calculator.

Does this apply to car or bike insurance?

No. Vehicle insurance has no surrender value. On cancellation (for example, after selling the vehicle), the insurer refunds the premium for the unused period minus a small charge. This guide is about life insurance only.
