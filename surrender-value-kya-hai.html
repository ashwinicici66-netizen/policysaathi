Surrender value, in simple words

Closing a life insurance policy before it matures is called surrendering it. The money the insurer pays back at that point is the surrender value. Here is the whole idea in a two-minute read.

In one line: Closing a life policy before maturity = surrender. The amount the insurer pays you at that moment = surrender value. It is almost always less than the premiums you have paid.

The quick answer

  • When it starts: after 2 full years of premiums on a regular premium policy. Surrender in year 1 and the guaranteed amount is ₹0.
  • How much: a percentage of the total premiums paid — 30% in year 2, 35% in year 3, 50% in years 4 to 7, rising to at least 90% in the last two years.
  • Who decides: IRDAI sets the minimum (GSV). Your insurer may pay more (SSV). You get whichever is higher.
  • Applies to: life insurance only — LIC, HDFC Life, ICICI Prudential, SBI Life and every other life insurer. Not car, bike or health insurance.

The two numbers you will see

1. GSV — Guaranteed Surrender Value

This is the legal minimum. IRDAI’s Insurance Products Regulations, 2024 fix the percentage an insurer must pay, based on total premiums paid (less any survival benefit already paid to you):

Policy year of surrenderMinimum GSV
1st year0% — no guaranteed value
2nd year30% of premiums paid
3rd year35% of premiums paid
4th–7th year50% of premiums paid
After the 7th yearRises progressively
Last 2 policy yearsAt least 90%

2. SSV — Special Surrender Value

The insurer calculates this itself from the value it has actually built up for you — bonuses, remaining years and expenses. SSV is usually higher than GSV, and since 2024 it can carry value from the first year itself.

The insurer pays whichever of the two is higher. So treat the GSV number as the floor, never as the final answer. Ask for a “surrender quotation” — insurers give it free.

A worked example

Ramesh has a 15-year endowment policy with a ₹50,000 annual premium. After 5 years he needs money:

  • Total premiums paid = ₹2,50,000
  • Year 5 falls in the 4–7 year bracket → minimum 50%
  • GSV = ₹1,25,000
  • If the insurer’s SSV works out to ₹1,45,000, Ramesh receives ₹1,45,000
Paying ₹2,50,000 and getting back ₹1,25,000–₹1,45,000 means surrendering usually locks in a loss. So ask yourself one question first: do I need cash right now, or can I simply stop paying and keep the policy alive?

Check the policy loan first

Most traditional policies allow a loan of roughly 80–90% of the surrender value. The advantage: you get the cash, the policy stays in force, and your life cover continues. Interest applies, but for a short-term need this usually beats surrendering.

If you do not need cash at all, paid-up is generally better than surrender: premiums stop, the sum assured is reduced, and the reduced amount is still paid at maturity. See the full comparison of surrender vs paid-up vs lapse.

How to surrender, step by step

  1. Collect the surrender form from the insurer’s website or branch
  2. Submit the form with the original policy document, bank details and KYC (PAN, Aadhaar)
  3. The insurer calculates the value and sends you a quotation
  4. Accept the quotation — the money reaches your bank in 7–15 working days

What surrendering costs you

  • A large part of the premiums paid, especially in the early years
  • Your life cover — and buying cover again later costs more because you are older
  • Continuity of tax benefits under section 80C (check the conditions on what you already claimed)
  • The financial goal the policy was meant to fund

Questions people ask

Is surrender value taxable?

For traditional policies the surrender value is generally not taxable if the policy met the premium-to-sum-assured conditions and you claimed section 80C within the limits. For ULIPs, plans with an annual premium above ₹1.25 lakh (₹2.5 lakh for policies issued on or after 1 April 2023, as per current rules) have taxable gains. This is general information — confirm your position with a CA.

What are my options instead of surrendering?

Three: surrender (cash now, policy and cover end), paid-up (premiums stop, reduced cover continues, reduced payout at maturity), and lapse (premiums stop, usually nothing is payable). A fourth option worth checking is a policy loan. Full comparison here.

What do I get if I surrender a ULIP?

Before 5 years: the IRDAI minimum table (GSV), less any discontinuation charge. After 5 years: the current fund value of your units, which may be higher or lower than the premiums you paid, depending on the market.

Where is the surrender value table in my policy document?

In the last pages, under a section titled “Surrender Value” or “Non-forfeiture benefits”. It lists the factor or amount for each policy year. That table — not this website — is the authoritative figure for your policy.

Does this apply to car, bike or health insurance?

No. Motor and health policies have no surrender value. If you cancel a motor policy you get a refund of the premium for the unused period, less a small cancellation charge; health premiums cover the policy year and are not refunded.
